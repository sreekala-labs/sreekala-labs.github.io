**AirTags**

I always wondered how Apple AirTags work internally. What is the technology behind it?

Apple AirTags are used for tracking without any cellular data, GPS, or subscription relying entirely on Apple's massive design Fleet.

**Hardware Internals:**

* Bluetooth LE(BLE) Radio: The only radio in the device. No GPS, No Cellular Data. 
* U1 Ultra-Wideband (UWB) chip: Enables precision finding when you're within ~30 ft of an iPhone with a U1 chip.
* Accelerometer : Used for playsound motion detection and to detect that the tag has moved.
* NFC chip: Lets anyone tap a lost AirTag with a phone to see "Lost Mode" contact info, no app needed.
* CR2032 Battery: This can give a battery life of up to 1 year.

Please note: Since there's no GPS, the AirTag itself never computes or transmits its own ocation, the location is truly from the nearby iPhones.

System Design:

1. The AirTag generates a public/private key-pair. It continuously broadcasts a rotating public key over BLE
   (changing roughly every 15 minutes) instead of a static MAC-like identifier. 
   This is the core anit-tracking design choice. Only the ownder's devices know the sequence of keys, derived from a shared secret
   set during pairing.

2. Any iPhone/iPad in the Find My network (regardless of owner) that's within BLE range passively detects the AirTag's beacon.
   That "finder" phone:

      * Determines its own approximate GPS location
      * Encrypts that location using the AirTag's currently broadcast public key (which the finder can't tie back to any identity)
      * Uploads the encrypted blob to Apple's servers, tagged only by a hash of the public key
  
3. Apple stores encrypted location reports keyed by public-key hash. Apple cannot decrypt them. It doesn't have the private key.
   It also can't tell who the finder was in any meaningful way, and can't tell which physical object the reports belong to.

4. The owner's iPhone, knowing the AirTag's private key and the key-rotation schedule, periodically queries Apple's servers
   for reports matching the hashes of keys it knows the tag recently broadcast.
   It downloads and decrypts them locally — only the owner can ever read the plaintext location.

5. There's no persistent connection. It's a store-and-forward system: report gets written once by a random finder,
   read once by the owner. This is why location updates are opportunistic, dependent on Find My network density and not real-time streaming.
